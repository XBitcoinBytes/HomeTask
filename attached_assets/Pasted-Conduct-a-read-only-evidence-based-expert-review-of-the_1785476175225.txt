PRODUCT NON-NEGOTIABLES

HomeTask should remain:

- vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript
- local-first
- installable as an iPhone PWA
- extremely simple during daily use
- understandable to an older user
- accessible with VoiceOver
- usable with large text
- compatible with Reduce Motion
- optimized for Safari and Home Screen installation
- safe with locally stored household history
- visually premium, liquid-metallic, calm, and readable
- functional offline after successful installation and initial loading

Do not recommend React, Vue, Next.js, TypeScript, a backend, synchronization, accounts, push notifications, or a framework migration merely because they are common industry choices.

Recommend architectural expansion only when a verified product requirement justifies it.

PRIMARY PRODUCT QUESTION

HomeTask’s main daily question should be:

“What Do I Need To Do Today?”

Powerful setup and editing features may exist, but they must not make everyday completion confusing.

KNOWN OWNER FINDINGS

Independently verify these findings rather than assuming they are correct:

- HomeTask needs a safe Home destination.
- Home and Today counts may disagree.
- All Tasks can dominate the experience.
- Future tasks are too prominent.
- “122 Tasks” should be actionable.
- Skip Today and Skip Week should use Title Case.
- Skip controls should not appear on future tasks.
- Search has produced physical-iPhone positioning problems.
- Search can allow excessive blank-space scrolling.
- Search can leave the background page scrollable.
- deleting the final search character can cause a dark or broken state
- the Search icon or decoration can resemble a broken green parenthesis
- Search results may not be actionable
- room names need strong hierarchy
- users cannot yet add, edit, move, archive, or delete tasks
- users cannot yet add, rename, archive, or delete rooms or zones
- users need seasonal, monthly, annual, and interval-based recurrence
- user-entered names must follow the Title Case policy
- a future template library should make custom entry uncommon
- the visual design is promising but does not yet fully achieve the intended premium liquid-metallic quality
- the product should eventually be safe to share with friends and family
- hotel or staff use is a possible future direction but may require a different multi-user architecture

Do not treat every requested feature as automatically correct. Challenge anything that would undermine simplicity, accessibility, reliability, or maintainability.

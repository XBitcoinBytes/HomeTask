Conduct an independent, evidence-based expert review of the current HomeTask product and then create a gated five-step roadmap.

PUBLIC PRODUCT

https://web-starter-kit-shipouts.replit.app/

IMPORTANT ROLE CLARIFICATION

Simulate a multidisciplinary panel of senior experts. The panel includes three senior engineers with deep Apple-platform, iPhone, Safari, WebKit, PWA, and Human Interface Guidelines experience.

These are expert review roles—not claims that the reviewers are currently employed by Apple or speaking for Apple.

PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Determine:

1. What HomeTask does well.
2. What is confusing, broken, fragile, inaccessible, or unsafe.
3. Whether HomeTask is worth continuing.
4. The five highest-value next steps.
5. The correct order in which to execute them.
6. How each step should be implemented, tested, approved, and deployed without breaking existing behavior.

DO NOT MODIFY ANYTHING YET

The initial review phase is strictly read-only.

During the initial review:

- do not edit files
- do not create files
- do not install packages
- do not change project configuration
- do not modify storage
- do not mark real tasks Done
- do not Skip real tasks
- do not import or restore data
- do not trigger destructive recovery paths against real data
- do not deploy
- do not republish
- do not accept Replit-generated task plans
- do not begin implementing the roadmap

Inspect the source, production build, existing tests, service worker, manifest, storage architecture, and public interface. Use isolated test data or mocks for anything that could modify user state.

After presenting the audit, debate, vote, and roadmap, STOP and ask for explicit approval to begin Step 1.

PRODUCT CONSTRAINTS

HomeTask must remain:

- vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript
- a local-first installable iPhone PWA
- extremely simple for an older user
- accessible with VoiceOver and large text
- usable with reduced motion
- optimized for iPhone Safari and Home Screen installation
- safe with locally stored household history
- visually premium, liquid-metallic, calm, and readable
- free of frameworks unless the owner explicitly authorizes reconsideration later

Do not recommend React, Vue, Next.js, TypeScript, a backend, accounts, synchronization, push notifications, or a framework migration merely because they are common choices.

Recommend those only if evidence demonstrates that a specific validated requirement cannot reasonably be met by the existing architecture.

EXECUTION PROTOCOL

After presenting the audit, debate, vote, and five-step roadmap:

STOP.

Do not implement Step 1.

Ask:

“Do you approve Roadmap Step 1 exactly as scoped, or would you like to revise it?”

Only after explicit owner approval may implementation begin.

When Step 1 is approved:

1. Restate its scope and non-goals.
2. Explain the implementation plan in plain language.
3. Identify data, migration, and rollback risks.
4. Ask for any required destructive-action confirmation.
5. Implement only Step 1.
6. Run its automated tests.
7. Test the development build.
8. Present the results.
9. Do not publish until the owner explicitly approves publication.
10. After publication, verify the public URL.
11. Present physical-iPhone retest instructions.
12. Stop and ask for approval before beginning Step 2.

Repeat the same approval gate for Steps 2–5.

At no point may approval of one step be interpreted as approval of later steps.

COMMUNICATION STYLE

- Be direct.
- Be specific.
- Avoid vague praise.
- Explain technical findings in understandable language.
- Do not bury critical defects.
- Do not overwhelm the owner with unnecessary implementation jargon.
- Preserve minority opinions.
- Clearly label verified facts versus hypotheses.
- Prefer product clarity and data safety over novelty.
